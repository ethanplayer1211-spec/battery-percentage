Measure voltage and current

The phone draws current through a tiny resistor called a shunt. The more current flows, the bigger the voltage drop across that resistor. The gauge measures the drop and uses the known resistance to work out the current.

Look up the starting charge

The gauge starts with an earlier reading taken while the battery was resting. This is called open-circuit voltage. It finds a table entry on either side of that voltage and estimates the starting charge between them.

Track how much charge flowed out

The gauge keeps a log of the current drawn throughout the run. It multiplies current by time for each interval and adds up the charge used. This is called coulomb counting.

    Calculate the percentage inside the gauge

    The gauge compares the charge left with what the battery can hold when full. That fraction is the state of charge. The purple mark shows where the estimate started, and the filled bar shows what remains.

    Key terms

    Fuel gauge
    Chip that estimates how much charge remains and reports it to the phone.
    Shunt resistor
    Known resistance in the power path. Current equals voltage drop divided by resistance.
    Voltage sag
    The cell voltage dips under load and can recover when current stops.
    Full-charge capacity
    The estimated charge a full battery can hold. Real gauges can adjust this estimate as the battery ages.

    Resting-voltage lookup table

    Nine lookup points derived from Analog Devices’ measurements of one lithium-ion cell. The gauge estimates between the two rows that bracket the reading.
